## Questions

### 1. Which sampling frequencies represented the 7 kHz signal correctly?

The 24 kHz and 16 kHz sampling frequencies represented the 7 kHz signal correctly. Their Nyquist frequencies were 12 kHz and 8 kHz, which are both higher than 7 kHz.

### 2. When did the 7 kHz signal appear as another frequency?

The 7 kHz signal appeared as another frequency when the sampling frequency was 12 kHz and 8 kHz.

* At 12 kHz sampling, 7 kHz appeared as 5 kHz.
* At 8 kHz sampling, 7 kHz appeared as 1 kHz.

This is called aliasing.

### 3. What happened when the Nyquist frequency became lower than 7 kHz?

When the Nyquist frequency became lower than 7 kHz, the signal could not be represented correctly. It appeared as a lower frequency instead of the original 7 kHz.

### 4. Did the aliased signal sound different?

Yes. The difference was more noticeable at 8 kHz because the 7 kHz signal became a 1 kHz signal. At 12 kHz, the signal became 5 kHz, so the difference in sound was smaller.

### 5. Why can MATLAB not recover the original 7 kHz after aliasing?

After aliasing, the original frequency information is lost. The samples of the 7 kHz signal become indistinguishable from the samples of the lower alias frequency. Therefore, MATLAB cannot know that the original signal was 7 kHz without additional information.

## Conclusion

The experiment showed that the sampling frequency must be at least twice the highest signal frequency. For a 7 kHz signal, the sampling frequency should be at least 14 kHz. When the sampling frequency was below 14 kHz, aliasing occurred and the signal appeared at a different, lower frequency.
